# Why? panel worked examples

Open when drafting or reviewing Why wording. All numbers are illustrative placeholders, not real market data, backtest results or current lot sizes. Real panels fill every value from Recommendation fields. Capital in examples is ₹1,00,000 with 1% risk (₹1,000).

## 1. Swing equity, low evidence

```
Risk first: you could lose about ₹960 (0.96% of your capital) on this trade.
Size: 80 shares. Entry ₹412 to ₹415. Stop ₹402. Target ₹438 (reward is about 2 times the risk).

The setup: the stock has pulled back for four days inside a larger uptrend.
Why now: it bounced off its 50-day average; volume is picking up; its sector is also strong.
Market today: uptrend with calm volatility. This setup likes that kind of market.
Proven wrong if: the price closes below ₹402. Then we exit and accept the loss.

Track record (pullback-in-uptrend on this stock, after costs): 22 trades, 9 won,
profit factor 1.4, worst drawdown 6%.  LOW EVIDENCE: fewer than 30 trades, so treat
this as a smaller, learning-size idea.

Watch out: quarterly results in 6 days. Hold time: about 5 to 10 days.
{DISCLAIMER}   (the shared constant; text owned by bz-india-markets compliance-and-disclaimer.md)
```

Why it works: rupee risk first, stop and invalidation named, low-evidence stated without dressing it up, event risk surfaced, no certainty words.

## 2. Options, defined risk, does not fit

```
No options trade suggested. One lot of this bull call spread can lose up to ₹4,100.
Your limit per trade is ₹1,000. Making it smaller would remove most of the point of the trade.
Market today: strong uptrend, IV rank low (options are cheap), so a debit spread suits the view.
What would change this: a cheaper, narrower structure whose maximum loss is under ₹1,000,
or a larger account. You can still study this trade on paper.
```

```
Best case ₹1,300 if the index stays below the sold strike at expiry. Breakeven: {level}.
Why: options are expensive today (IV rank 68), the index is stuck in a range, and the sold strike sits above a heavy resistance zone.
Proven wrong if: the index closes above {level}. We exit; no waiting for expiry.
Exit plan: take profit at about 60% of the best case, or exit 3 days before expiry.
Chance of profit is about 6 in 10 by the pricing model (a model estimate, not a promise).
```
